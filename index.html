An International Forum for Critical Thinking

A one-day academic workshop bringing together students from different countries to examine complex global questions, debate opposing perspectives, and transform evidence into ideas.

One Question. Two Perspectives. One Academic Challenge.

Every participant works on the same carefully selected question in economics, public policy, or global affairs. What differs is the side of the argument they are asked to build — and the rigour with which they must build it.

Pro

Defends the proposition

One group is responsible for the strongest possible defence of the selected idea, policy, or proposition. They establish what the claim actually asserts, gather the evidence that supports it, and answer the objections it will face.

Contra

Challenges the proposition

The second group builds the strongest possible case against it. They test the reasoning, examine the quality of the evidence, expose the assumptions it depends on, and propose what a better answer might look like.

  • Research

    Positions begin in the literature and the data, not in opinion.

  • Evidence

    Every claim is traced back to a source that can be examined.

  • Academic reasoning

    Arguments are structured, explicit, and open to scrutiny.

  • Counterarguments

    Each group must state the other side's best case before answering it.

The Day, Step by Step

A single day, structured so that each stage produces the material for the next. Nothing is improvised: students arrive prepared, and leave with a draft.

  1. 01

    Research

    Before the workshop, each group assembles sources, data, and prior arguments on the selected question, with guidance on what counts as reliable evidence.

  2. 02

    Question

    Mentors and peers interrogate the material: what is being claimed, what would falsify it, and which assumptions are doing the real work.

  3. 03

    Debate

    The two groups meet in a structured, timed exchange. Each side must represent the opposing case accurately before responding to it.

  4. 04

    Write

    Each group converts its position into one collaborative academic article: a clear thesis, documented evidence, and an honest treatment of its own limitations.

  5. 05

    Publish

    Drafts are reviewed with academic mentors and revised. We hope to explore publication through an appropriate academic platform, subject to editorial approval.

What Students Take Away

The workshop is designed around four capacities that transfer well beyond a single day and a single question.

Critical Thinking

Separating what a source demonstrates from what it merely asserts.

Evidence-Based Debate

Disagreeing precisely, in public, without abandoning the evidence.

Academic Writing

Turning a spoken argument into structured, citable prose.

International Collaboration

Building one argument with people whose starting assumptions differ.

Two Articles. Two Perspectives.

The workshop culminates in two collaborative academic articles, each presenting a carefully researched and reasoned perspective on the same question.

Read together, the two articles are the real output of the day: a record of where a disagreement genuinely lies, what evidence each side rests on, and which questions remain open. Both articles credit the full group that produced them.

Potential publication through an appropriate ETH Zürich academic platform would be subject to academic and editorial review.
