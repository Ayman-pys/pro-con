One Question. Two Perspectives. One Academic Challenge.

Every participant works on the same carefully selected question in economics, public policy, or global affairs. What differs is the side of the argument they are asked to build — and the rigour with which they must build it.

Pro

Defends the proposition

One group is responsible for the strongest possible defence of the selected idea, policy, or proposition. They establish what the claim actually asserts, gather the evidence that supports it, and answer the objections it will face.

Contra

Challenges the proposition

The second group builds the strongest possible case against it. They test the reasoning, examine the quality of the evidence, expose the assumptions it depends on, and propose what a better answer might look like.

  • Research

    Positions begin in the literature and the data, not in opinion.

  • Evidence

    Every claim is traced back to a source that can be examined.

  • Academic reasoning

    Arguments are structured, explicit, and open to scrutiny.

  • Counterarguments

    Each group must state the other side's best case before answering it.

The 7-Day Program

Seven days allow participants to move beyond a single debate and experience the full research process — from defining a question and evaluating evidence to academic writing, peer review, and final presentation.

Research → Debate → Write → Review → Present → Refine

  1. 1

    Day 1

    Topic & Research Question

    • Choosing the topic
    • Defining the research question
    • Understanding your Pro / Contra position
  2. 2

    Day 2

    Research & Sources

    • Searching for academic sources
    • Evaluating source credibility
    • Gathering evidence and data
  3. 3

    Day 3

    Evidence & Argumentation

    • Analysing the evidence
    • Building arguments
    • Distinguishing evidence from opinion
    • Identifying strengths and weaknesses in each position
  4. 4

    Day 4

    Debate & Critical Discussion

    • Presenting the Pro and Contra arguments
    • Discussing the evidence
    • Mutual critique
    • Refining arguments based on feedback
  5. 5

    Day 5

    Academic Writing

    • Structuring the research paper
    • Writing the introduction
    • Developing the arguments
    • Using sources and citations correctly
  6. 6

    Day 6

    Review & Revision

    • Reviewing the research
    • Checking sources and citations
    • Improving methodology and argumentation
    • Peer review among participants
  7. 7

    Day 7

    Final Presentation

    • Presenting the final research
    • Sharing findings and arguments
    • Q&A discussion
    • Final feedback
    • Preparing manuscripts that may be further revised for academic submission

Completing the seven days does not guarantee that a manuscript will be published. Any further revision, review, or academic submission takes place after the program and is not something this initiative can promise in advance.

Guided by Academic Mentorship

The initiative aims to involve at least four professors or academic researchers, who would guide students through research, discussion, argument development, and writing. Mentors will be invited, and their participation is subject to availability and institutional approval. No mentor has been confirmed at this stage.

What Students Take Away

The program is designed around four capacities that transfer well beyond the seven days and a single question.

Critical Thinking

Separating what a source demonstrates from what it merely asserts.

Evidence-Based Debate

Disagreeing precisely, in public, without abandoning the evidence.

Academic Writing

Turning a spoken argument into structured, citable prose.

International Collaboration

Building one argument with people whose starting assumptions differ.

Two Articles. Two Perspectives.

The program culminates in two collaborative research manuscripts, each presenting a carefully researched and reasoned perspective on the same question.

Read together, the two manuscripts are the real output of the program: a record of where a disagreement genuinely lies, what evidence each side rests on, and which questions remain open. Both are credited to the full group that produced them.

Any manuscript may be further revised for potential academic submission; publication through an ETH Zürich platform, or any other venue, is not guaranteed and would be subject to academic and editorial review.
